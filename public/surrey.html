Security doors and access control across Surrey

Security is a particularly live driver of door work in Surrey, across every sector. For multi-family buildings, security is a system: secured main-entry doors with electronic access control, parkade and bike-room doors on fobs or credentials, amenity access tied to the building's system, and suite entries specified to resist forced entry while still meeting egress and fire code. Electronic access also changes the door and frame prep long before hardware arrives: power transfer at the hinge, prep for the strike or mag-lock, and coordination with the low-voltage contractor all get resolved at the door schedule. Just Doors specifies security and access-controlled doors as complete, code-satisfying assemblies — reinforced where it counts, electrified where the building needs it, always compliant with egress.

Fire-rated and fireproof doors in Surrey

Fire-door compliance is where fast-moving Surrey projects most often lose time, and it's avoidable with the right assembly and paperwork. Under the BC Building Code, garage-to-house doors in townhomes and single-family require a fire-rated, self-closing assembly; multi-family suite entries carry 20- to 45-minute ratings with self-closing and positive-latching hardware and smoke seals; and stairwells, shafts, and high-hazard service and industrial rooms escalate to 90-minute, 1-hour, and 2-hour assemblies. The ratings are straightforward — what fails inspection is the field condition of the assembly.

A fire door is a tested assembly, not just a leaf. The label on the door, the label on the frame, the hinges, the closer, and the latching hardware all have to match a listed configuration, and NFPA 80 governs the field conditions: the perimeter gap, the maximum undercut, positive latching on closing, and the prohibition on field modifications outside the manufacturer's listing. Surrey Fire Service checks these. A closer that drifts instead of latching, an undercut trimmed too far for flooring, or a hinge swapped for a lighter residential unit on a rated leaf fails the opening and forces a re-inspection that holds up occupancy. Just Doors supplies fire assemblies as coordinated, labelled packages — leaf, frame, and hardware listed together — with the documentation Surrey inspectors look for, so the general contractor isn't assembling a compliant opening from parts sourced three different ways.

In townhouse and low-rise work, the same discipline applies at demising-wall and shared-corridor doors, keeping sound where it belongs between units.

The Fraser Valley climate and door performance

Surrey sits in the Fraser Valley, and while it's marginally less exposed than the coast, it's still a wet climate with real humidity swings — and buildings under construction, before HVAC is commissioned and the envelope is sealed, move through wide moisture ranges. Wood doors hung into that environment can absorb moisture, swell, and bind, or later dry and shrink once the building is conditioned, opening gaps that fail acoustic and smoke seals. The discipline is to acclimatize doors to the building's eventual moisture content before hanging, and to select moisture-stable cores with properly sealed top and bottom edges — particularly on exterior-adjacent openings and in any building without commissioned climate control. On a fast Surrey schedule, this is the difference between doors that operate cleanly at handover and a callback list of binding leaves in the first wet season.

Door hardware and access control for Surrey buildings

Hardware is where a door package either supports a building's operation or becomes a maintenance liability. Surrey door FAQ

Does Surrey follow the same door code as Vancouver? No. Surrey follows the British Columbia Building Code, administered by the City of Surrey's building department with fire review through Surrey Fire Service, whereas the City of Vancouver uses its own standalone Vancouver Building By-law. Door fire ratings and assemblies should be verified against the BC Building Code and Surrey's requirements for Surrey projects.

Do townhouse garage-to-house doors need to be fire-rated in Surrey? Generally yes. The BC Building Code requires the door between an attached garage and the living space to be a fire-rated, self-closing, tightly sealed assembly. It's one of the most common openings on a Surrey townhouse project and a frequent inspection item, so it needs to be specified and installed as a listed assembly, not a standard interior door.

Can Just Doors standardize a door package across a multi-building townhouse project? Yes — and it's where we add the most value in Surrey. We specify the door package once against your unit plans, then repeat it consistently across every building: the same hardware groups, ratings, and finishes, with the rated openings handled correctly on every unit. That standardization compresses procurement and inspection risk across the whole development.

What fire rating do multi-family suite entries need in Surrey? Suite entry doors typically require a certified 20- to 45-minute fire-protection rating with self-closing hardware, positive latching, and smoke seals; stairwells, shafts, and high-hazard rooms escalate higher. The exact rating depends on the separation and occupancy, and the full assembly — leaf, frame, and hardware — must match a listed configuration to pass Surrey Fire Service review.

Do you handle security and access-controlled doors for Surrey buildings? Yes.
