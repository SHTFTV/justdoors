Security doors and access control across Burnaby

Security is a live driver of door work across Burnaby's sectors. For residential and townhouse clients, that means reinforced entry doors with multi-point locking, solid or steel-reinforced cores, and strike reinforcement that resists forced entry. For multi-family towers — the bulk of Burnaby's growth — security is a system: secured main-entry doors with electronic access control, parkade and bike-room doors on fobs or credentials, amenity access tied to the building's system, and suite entries specified to resist forced entry while still meeting egress and fire code. For commercial and industrial tenancies in Big Bend and the town-centre podiums, it runs from storefront security to secured warehouse man-doors, records and server-room access, and after-hours lockdown hardware.

The specification challenge is that security hardware has to coexist with life-safety code. A secured stairwell or exit door still has to allow free egress under the BC Building Code and NFPA 101 — you cannot lock people in — so electrified hardware, delayed-egress devices, and access-controlled openings must satisfy both the security goal and the egress requirement at once. Electronic access also changes the door and frame prep before hardware arrives: power transfer at the hinge, prep for the strike or mag-lock, and coordination with the low-voltage contractor, all resolved at the door schedule. Just Doors specifies security and access-controlled doors as complete, code-satisfying assemblies — reinforced where it counts, electrified where the building needs it, always compliant with egress.

Fire-rated and fireproof doors in Burnaby

Fire-door compliance is where fast-moving Burnaby tower projects most often lose time, and it's avoidable with the right assembly and paperwork. Under the BC Building Code, multi-family suite entries carry 20- to 45-minute ratings with self-closing and positive-latching hardware and smoke seals; townhouse garage-to-house doors require a fire-rated, self-closing assembly; and stairwells, shafts, and high-hazard service and industrial rooms escalate to 90-minute, 1-hour, and 2-hour assemblies. The ratings are straightforward — what fails inspection is the field condition of the assembly.

A fire door is a tested assembly, not just a leaf. The label on the door, the label on the frame, the hinges, the closer, and the latching hardware all have to match a listed configuration, and NFPA 80 governs the field conditions: the perimeter gap, the maximum undercut, positive latching on closing, and the prohibition on field modifications outside the manufacturer's listing. Burnaby Fire Department checks these. A closer that drifts instead of latching, an undercut trimmed too far, or a residential hinge on a rated leaf fails the opening and forces a re-inspection that holds up occupancy. Just Doors supplies fire assemblies as coordinated, labelled packages — leaf, frame, and hardware listed together — with the documentation Burnaby inspectors look for.

Acoustic doors for Burnaby's tower density

Burnaby's town-centre towers stack hundreds of suites, and acoustic performance is an expectation, not a nicety — and doors are the weak point in an otherwise isolated suite. A developer can build a party wall to a strong Sound Transmission Class rating and still field complaints if the suite entry leaks corridor noise: footfall, elevator sound, and mechanical hum from shared spaces. Just Doors specifies acoustic suite entries as a system — a solid core selected for its STC contribution, full perimeter gasketing, and an automatic drop seal at the sill — so the assembled opening stays close to the surrounding wall's rating. In townhouse and low-rise work, the same discipline applies at demising-wall and shared-corridor doors.

The coastal climate and door performance in Burnaby

Burnaby shares the Lower Mainland's wet coastal climate, and buildings under construction — before HVAC is commissioned and the envelope is sealed — move through wide humidity swings. Wood doors hung into that environment can absorb moisture, swell, and bind, or later dry and shrink once the building is conditioned, opening gaps that fail acoustic and smoke seals. The discipline is to acclimatize doors to the building's eventual moisture content before hanging, and to select moisture-stable cores with properly sealed top and bottom edges — particularly on exterior-adjacent openings, mountain-top exposures at UniverCity, and any building without commissioned climate control. On a fast Burnaby schedule, this is the difference between doors that operate cleanly at handover and a callback list of binding leaves in the first wet season.

Door hardware and access control for Burnaby buildings

Hardware is where a door package either supports a building's operation or becomes a maintenance liability. Burnaby's towers increasingly run electronic access at amenity spaces, parkades, and sometimes suite doors, which changes the door and frame prep before hardware arrives. For code-governed openings, the hardware group has to satisfy the rating and the egress requirement simultaneously — rated suite entries need listed self-closing and positive-latching hardware; stairwell and exit doors frequently require single-motion panic devices with fire-rated strikes; and closers have to be sized to the door and the draft conditions, including the pressurized-shaft conditions common in tall towers. Just Doors builds the hardware group into the door schedule so the closer, hinge, lock, and seal are specified as a matched set for each opening type.

Burnaby door FAQ

Does Burnaby follow the same door code as Vancouver? No. Burnaby follows the British Columbia Building Code, administered by the City of Burnaby's building department with fire review through Burnaby Fire Department, whereas the City of Vancouver uses its own standalone Vancouver Building By-law. Door fire ratings and assemblies should be verified against the BC Building Code and Burnaby's requirements for Burnaby projects.

Can Just Doors standardize a door package across a master-planned tower community? Yes — and it's where we add the most value in Burnaby's Brentwood, Lougheed, and Metrotown communities. We specify the suite-entry and common-area door package once against the tower plans, then repeat it consistently across every building in the master plan: the same hardware groups, ratings, and finishes, with rated openings handled correctly on every floor. That standardization compresses procurement and inspection risk across the whole development.

What fire rating do multi-family suite entries need in Burnaby? Suite entry doors typically require a certified 20- to 45-minute fire-protection rating with self-closing hardware, positive latching, and smoke seals; stairwells, shafts, and high-hazard rooms escalate higher. The exact rating depends on the separation and occupancy, and the full assembly — leaf, frame, and hardware — must match a listed configuration to pass Burnaby Fire Department review.

Do townhouse garage-to-house doors need to be fire-rated in Burnaby? Generally yes. The BC Building Code requires the door between an attached garage and the living space to be a fire-rated, self-closing, tightly sealed assembly — a common opening on Burnaby townhouse projects and a frequent inspection item.

Do you handle industrial and commercial doors in Big Bend? Yes. We supply hollow-metal exterior and rated man-doors, warehouse and overhead-adjacent openings, commercial storefront entrances, and the fire separations that divide industrial and commercial tenancies — with commercial-grade hardware sized for heavy use.
