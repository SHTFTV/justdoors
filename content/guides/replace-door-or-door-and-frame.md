# Replace the Door Only, or the Door and Frame?

**The right replacement scope depends on the existing opening, the new door’s requirements and the work needed around it. Check the frame, hardware interfaces and surrounding finishes before pricing a leaf-only replacement.**

Replacing only the moving door leaf can sound like the simplest option. It may be appropriate for some openings, but retaining the frame is a decision to verify rather than assume. If the underlying problem is a damaged frame, poor alignment or an incompatible assembly, replacing the leaf alone may leave the original problem unresolved.

## Describe the problem before selecting a product

Explain whether the concern is appearance, damage, operation, weather exposure, security or a change in how the space is used. Note when the problem occurs and what has already been adjusted or repaired. A door that does not close reliably deserves an assessment of the opening and hardware, not simply a new panel selected from a photograph.

Photograph both sides of the opening, the frame, the threshold and visible hardware. Include wider views showing adjacent walls and finishes. Label the images with the location so the contractor can distinguish similar openings in a multi-unit property. Do not remove hardware or disturb labels to obtain a picture.

## Have the retained frame assessed

Ask the installer to inspect frame condition, alignment, anchorage and the existing preparations relevant to the proposed door. Corrosion, impact damage or an opening that is out of square may affect whether retaining the frame is practical. The surrounding wall construction also affects what is involved in frame replacement.

Confirm who will measure the opening and approve the dimensions for manufacture. Existing hinge and lock locations can matter just as much as height and width. A product described with the same nominal size is not necessarily ready to hang in the existing frame without further coordination.

## Review hardware and required performance

List the hardware to be retained and the hardware to be replaced. Ask how the proposed door will interface with existing locks, closers, access control, seals and glazing. A change in weight, material or intended use can affect the package that needs to be reviewed.

For openings with specified fire, acoustic, security or accessibility requirements, have the responsible project professionals confirm the proposed assembly. Do not assume a new leaf automatically preserves the performance of the original opening. Keep existing labels and available records visible for assessment, and obtain product-specific documentation for the proposed replacement.

## Price the work around the opening

A frame replacement may involve removal and reinstatement of trim, wall finishes, paint, flooring transitions or exterior weather detailing. Ask the quote to identify these tasks and who completes them. For occupied premises, discuss temporary access, protection and the sequence between removal and completion.

In a strata or leased building, confirm the approval process and responsibility for the opening before ordering. Share written building requirements and arrange access to any shared areas. Treat delivery dates, site readiness and the installation appointment as separate coordination items.

## Compare two clearly defined scopes

If both approaches remain viable after inspection, request a leaf-only option and a complete-opening option with their assumptions written down. Compare the final result, the excluded work and the risk of further repairs rather than only the initial material price. Ask what conditions discovered during removal would trigger a change.

Keep the selected quote, approved dimensions, product information and completion records together.
