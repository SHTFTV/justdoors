## Why Vancouver door packages are their own animal

Most Lower Mainland municipalities adopt the BC Building Code with local amendments layered on top. Vancouver does not. The VBBL is a standalone bylaw administered by the City's Development, Buildings & Licensing department out of 515 W 10th Ave, and its provisions around fire separations, suite-entry protection, and corridor smoke control are frequently more prescriptive than the provincial code. For door work, three consequences matter.

First, the fire-rating expectations are tighter and more explicitly enforced. Vancouver Fire and Rescue Services (VFRS) has a well-established review posture on suite entry doors, stairwell shaft assemblies, and service-room separations, and inspectors here will ask for the certification label, the gap and undercut measurements, and evidence of positive latching under pressure. A door leaf that carries the right core but was field-modified — a lock prep drilled outside the manufacturer's listing, a bottom trimmed past the allowable undercut — fails the assembly, not just the hardware.

Second, the density of high-rise and mixed-use podium construction means Vancouver projects carry far more rated openings per floor than a typical suburban build. A downtown residential tower floorplate can involve dozens of 20- to 45-minute suite entries, 90-minute to 2-hour stairwell and shaft doors, electrical and mechanical room separations, and refuse/recycling chute access doors — each with its own rating, hardware group, and inspection checkpoint. That volume is exactly why a coordinated door schedule, rather than piecemeal ordering, saves general contractors weeks.

Third, Vancouver's permit and inspection sequencing is unforgiving of late door decisions. Rough openings framed for the wrong frame throat, missing wood backing at hinge and strike locations, or a door swing that conflicts with egress width all surface at inspection — long after the framing crew has moved on. Just Doors works from the door schedule backward into the rough-opening requirements so the frames, backing, and clearances are correct before the walls close.

## Fire-rated doors and the VFRS inspection reality

Fire door compliance is where Vancouver projects most often lose time, and it's entirely avoidable with the right assembly and paperwork. Under the VBBL, suite entry doors in multi-family residential typically carry a certified 20- to 45-minute fire-protection rating with self-closing hardware, positive latching, and smoke seals; stairwell enclosures, shafts, and high-hazard service rooms move up to 90-minute, 1-hour, and 2-hour assemblies depending on the separation. The ratings themselves are straightforward. What trips crews up is the field condition of the assembly at inspection.

A fire door is a *tested assembly*, not just a leaf. The label on the door, the label on the frame, the hinges, the closer, the latching hardware, and the clearances all have to match a listed configuration. NFPA 80 governs the field conditions: the perimeter gap tolerance, the maximum bottom undercut, the requirement that the door positively latch on closing, and the prohibition on field modifications outside the manufacturer's listing. Vancouver inspectors check these. A closer that lets the door drift instead of latching, an undercut trimmed for carpet that exceeds the allowable clearance, a hinge swapped for a lighter residential unit on a heavy rated leaf — any one of these fails the opening and forces a re-inspection that can hold up occupancy.

Just Doors supplies fire assemblies as coordinated, labelled packages — leaf, frame, and hardware group listed together — and provides the documentation VFRS looks for, so the general contractor isn't assembling a compliant opening from parts sourced three different ways. Where installation is handled by our Lower Mainland install partner, the frames are set to the correct clearances and the hardware is templated so the assembly passes on the first inspection rather than the third.

## Acoustic doors for Vancouver's strata density

Sound is a Vancouver-specific pressure because so much of the city's residential stock is stacked, stratified, and governed by noise bylaws and disclosure obligations. A developer can build a party wall to a high STC rating and still field complaints if the suite entry door leaks corridor noise — footfall, elevator dings, conversation, and mechanical hum from shared spaces. The door is the acoustic weak point unless it's specified deliberately.

For Vancouver multi-family, Just Doors specifies acoustic suite entries as a system: a solid mineral or particleboard core selected for its STC contribution, full perimeter gasketing at the head and jambs, and an automatic drop seal at the sill that engages when the door closes. The target is to keep the assembled opening within a few points of the surrounding wall so the door isn't the acoustic hole in an otherwise quiet suite. In renovation contexts — common in Yaletown and the West End — retrofitting these seals into existing frames requires field measurement, because older frames vary in throat and reveal, and an off-the-shelf seal kit rarely fits a 1980s door frame cleanly.

## The coastal climate problem nobody schedules for

Vancouver's marine climate is a genuine door-performance issue that suburban and interior projects don't share. Winter relative humidity on the coast routinely sits between 75% and 95%, and buildings under construction — before HVAC is commissioned and the envelope is sealed — swing through wide moisture ranges. Wood doors hung into that environment can absorb moisture, swell, and bind, or later dry and shrink once the building is conditioned, opening gaps that fail acoustic and smoke seals.

Two practices matter here. First, door storage and acclimatization on site: doors should be stored flat, sealed, and allowed to reach the building's eventual equilibrium moisture content before hanging, not pulled off a delivery truck and hung the same afternoon into an unconditioned floorplate. Second, edge and core selection: moisture-stable cores and properly sealed top and bottom edges resist the coastal swing far better than unsealed paint-grade blanks. On west-side residential near glazing and exterior thresholds, and on any door in a building without commissioned climate control, this is the difference between a door that operates cleanly at handover and a callback list of binding and gapping leaves in the first wet season.

## Vancouver door FAQ

**Do Vancouver projects follow the BC Building Code for doors?**
No — the City of Vancouver administers its own Vancouver Building By-law (VBBL 2019, 2024 amendments), not the provincial code. Door fire ratings, suite-entry protection, and corridor separations are governed by the VBBL and enforced by Development, Buildings & Licensing together with Vancouver Fire and Rescue Services. Assemblies acceptable under the BCBC elsewhere should be verified against the VBBL for Vancouver work.

**What fire rating do suite entry doors need in Vancouver multi-family buildings?**
Suite entry doors typically require a certified 20- to 45-minute fire-protection rating with self-closing hardware, positive latching, and smoke seals. Stairwell enclosures, shafts, and high-hazard service rooms escalate to 90-minute, 1-hour, or 2-hour assemblies. The exact rating depends on the separation and occupancy, and the assembly — leaf, frame, and hardware — must match a listed configuration to pass VFRS review.

**Why do fire doors fail inspection in Vancouver?**
Most failures are field-condition issues, not the wrong door: an undercut trimmed past the allowable clearance, a closer that doesn't positively latch the door, a hinge or lock prep outside the manufacturer's listing, or a missing label. A fire door is a tested assembly under NFPA 80, so every component and clearance has to match the listing. ## Energy Step Code, Zero Emissions, and door air-tightness

Vancouver has pushed harder on building energy performance than almost any Canadian jurisdiction, and that ambition lands directly on exterior and demising doors. The city's energy requirements — including its zero-emissions and Passive-House-influenced targets for many new buildings — put air-tightness front and centre. A high-performance envelope is only as tight as its openings, and every exterior door, parkade-to-lobby door, corridor-to-stair door, and amenity door is a potential leakage path that can undermine a whole-building air-tightness test.

For door work, this changes specification in concrete ways. Exterior and vestibule doors need continuous, durable weatherstripping and properly adjusted thresholds and sweeps, and on the tightest projects, door assemblies are selected and installed with the blower-door test in mind rather than treated as an afterthought. Automatic operators at accessible entries have to be coordinated so they don't defeat the air seal when idle. Where a building targets Passive House or an equivalently aggressive air-tightness metric, the entry assemblies — gaskets, sills, and operators — become part of the envelope strategy, and getting them wrong shows up as a failed test late in the schedule. Just Doors specifies exterior and separation doors with these targets in mind, so the openings support the air-tightness result instead of sabotaging it.

## Door systems in depth for Vancouver towers

Each door type carries its own role in a Vancouver building, and the specification logic is worth spelling out because the wrong product in the wrong opening is the most common source of change orders.

**Hollow metal doors and frames** are the workhorse of Vancouver's rated openings — stairwell enclosures, shaft and service-room separations, parkade access, and back-of-house. In concrete towers, welded hollow-metal frames anchored into the structure carry the 90-minute to 2-hour ratings that stairwells and shafts demand, and the leaves are specified in the appropriate gauge for the traffic and the rating. Galvannealed substrates matter in Vancouver's damp environment, particularly in parkades and exterior-adjacent conditions where uncoated steel corrodes. Factory priming and, increasingly, factory finishing let hollow metal appear in architectural colors where it fronts occupied space rather than back-of-house.

**Mineral-core and solid-core wood doors** dominate suite entries and interior openings in residential towers. Mineral-core leaves carry the 20- to 45-minute suite-entry ratings while accepting a wood veneer or paint-grade face that reads as a finished residential door. Core selection drives both the fire rating and the acoustic contribution, which is why suite entries in acoustically sensitive Yaletown or West End buildings are specified as a coordinated core-plus-seal system rather than a bare rated blank. Edge banding and sealing protect the leaf from the coastal moisture swing.

**Aluminum entrances and storefronts** appear at commercial suite thresholds, ground-floor retail, lobby vestibules, and creative-office receptions. Narrow-stile aluminum takes specific hardware — Adams Rite-style mortise latches and patch fittings rather than standard wood-door locksets — and where these entrances need a fire rating, the assembly moves to fire-rated aluminum framing with ceramic or specialty fire-resistive glazing. Thermal performance and air-tightness at exterior aluminum entries tie directly back to Vancouver's energy targets.

**Fire-rated glazed assemblies** solve the recurring Vancouver problem of maintaining sightlines and daylight across a fire separation — a demising line in an open creative office, a corridor that wants to feel open, a lobby separation. Rated ceramic and fire-resistive glazing in listed frames delivers the separation without a solid wall, and it lets heritage and design-forward projects keep their character while meeting code.

**Specialty and luxury doors** — oversized pivots at tower receptions, tall-format leaves for high-span residential ceilings, pocket and sliding assemblies in high-end suites — are where Vancouver's luxury market pushes past the standard catalogue. These are long-lead items, and specifying them late is a common cause of schedule slip. Flagging them at the takeoff stage is the difference between a smooth install and a floor waiting on a custom door.

## Certification, labelling, and the paper trail Vancouver inspectors want

A fire door's rating lives in its label, and in Vancouver the documentation is scrutinized as closely as the hardware.
